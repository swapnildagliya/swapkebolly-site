SANGAM

संगम  ·  Confluence

An all-female village. A river goddess. A rejected boy.
A world that has never held water — until it does.

Creative North Star

I

Ritual

Movement is meditative before it is explosive. Repetition, collective breathing, ensemble synchronisation. Stillness carries as much weight as the most complex choreography.

II

Spectacle

Strategic projections, fabric as landscape, lighting transformations. The blue dupatta is not a prop — it is the river. When it is pulled away, the audience watches a world die.

III

Emotional Arc

Every climactic moment must be earned through restraint. Joy is most devastating when someone is missing from it. The queen's sacrifice — her life — means nothing to an audience that has not watched her prison become her love.

Story arc

From rejection to reunion

Maha’s grief gives the village its river. The boy grows among them, is rejected again by the Queen, and returns changed through her sacrifice. The numbered dance scenes and working script carry the detailed sequence.

Characters

Cast of SANGAM

Five named characters carrying the dramatic arc of the show.

K

The Queen

Rani Kanō

रानी कनो  ·  Queen of the village

Played by Svetlana Bubnova

Arc

Rigid and fearful → catalyst for catastrophe → redeemed through ultimate sacrifice. She acts from terror, not malice. Control is her religion and her prison — until she dissolves both, offering her life to send her soul into the boy's heart. She does not live to see the world she heals.

Movement Vocabulary

Imperial, grounded, commanding. Early scenes: stillness that demands attention. As she unravels: increasingly desperate, frantic. Scene 16: bare, still — then, in the act of giving, she moves inward. The final gesture is not collapse. It is release. Her last movement is the quietest thing on the stage.

Costume Direction

White Anarkali is hers. Opening: the dressiest Anarkali on stage — big green kundan set, golden dupatta, tiara. In the village scenes she wears the village costume. The Appeal and Sangam: white Anarkali + scarf, big bindi and black dupatta at Sangam. Finale: white Anarkali.

S01 BirthS08 Feast → Rejection ◆S14 InvitationS16 Sacrifice ◆
M

The Boy

Manu

मनु  ·  The poet, the bridge

Played by Swapnil Dagliya

Arc

Hidden, innocent → gradually discovered and accepted → publicly rejected, heartbroken → transformative bridge to the village's healing. Seeks connection, not validation.

Movement Vocabulary

Gentle, fluid, responsive. Adult: quiet longing. Rejection: collapse or stillness — never rage. Appeal (S15): completely exposed, dancing with his entire being.

Costume Direction

Black dhoti and black necklace, oiled hair open with braids. Blue sherwani at First Discovery, Kalbeliya at the Feast, Dola Re costume with orange pants for Nataraja and Bolly Folk, yellow & white for Bhangra. The Appeal: pink skirt, pink sleeveless jacket, green jacket — swapped for the blue garba jacket at Sangam. Reunion: jacket off, Anarkali. Finale: neon jacket, pink skirt.

S04 AdulthoodS05 DiscoveryS15 Appeal ◆S16 Sacrifice → Becoming ◆S18 ReturnS19 Reunion with Uma
P

The Sun & The Moon

Prabha & Chaaya

प्रभा & छाया  ·  Sun & Moon · Light & Shadow

Played by Shreya Vaidya & Laurien De Ridder

Arc

Cosmic caretakers who protect the boy not through ownership but through unconditional presence. They do not own him — they shelter him. Their role is freedom, not possession.

Movement Vocabulary

Flowing, aerial, celestial. Veils suggest light and shadow, day and night. Near-synchronisation — two cosmic bodies orbiting the same centre. Rooted in Bollyfolk but with an ethereal, otherworldly quality.

Costume Direction

White sarees over a skirt as petticoat, silver leggings. Sun: yellow shiny embroidery, golden armband, golden earrings & set. Moon: black shiny embroidery, long silver necklace, silver-white earrings, silver armbands.

S02 Sun & MoonS04 Adulthood cameoS15 Villagers' Appeal
V

The Messenger

Vayu

वायु  ·  The Wind

Played by Narcisse Merlier

Arc

Neutral observer and active messenger between worlds. Carries the village's plea to the Three Tribes. Neither judge nor saviour — the medium through which cosmic forces communicate.

Movement Vocabulary

Fluid, directional, purposeful. The wind doesn't hesitate. Movement has clear intention and direction. Unlike Sun & Moon (ethereal) or the queen (grounded), Vayu is always in transit.

Costume Direction

Red saree, jingle belt, jingle silver blouse.

S10 Tribe InvitationS15 Appeal
U

The River Goddess / Mother

Uma

उमा  ·  The source of all water

Played by Srimahavalli

Arc

Mother of two children — the boy and the river. Both born the same moment, from the same grief. She creates the river to send her rejected baby downstream to Sun & Moon, who become his guardians. The village drinks her tears and calls it abundance — they never know. When the Queen rejects her child (S08), her heart breaks again and the river dies — there is no more baby to send away, the water has nowhere to go. The drought is not punishment. It is love withdrawn. The rain returns only when the Queen agrees and gives her soul into the boy (S16).

Movement Vocabulary

Grounded, maternal, fluid as water. S01: stillness, then weeping — and the dupatta unfurls from her body. She lays the baby on the river she just made. She does not collapse. She gives. Through Part 1: she carries the secret. Her hand stays at her heart while the village celebrates the abundance she paid for. Drought (S09): she simply stops flowing — no theatre, no scream. The river withdraws because she has nothing left. Finale (S17–19): she returns not with drama but with inevitability. Water always finds its level.

Costume Direction

White Anarkali — in the opening and at the reunion with the boy.
